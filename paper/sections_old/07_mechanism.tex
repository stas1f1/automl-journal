\section{Why more structure is not better}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The ordering in Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} does not follow architectural weight:
the most constrained system performs worst and the best of the three sits between
the extremes. Quantity of structure does not predict outcome. We propose a
sharper statement, which the data can test:

\begin{quote}
Constraint pays exactly where it removes a decision the weak model reliably
gets wrong. Everywhere else it removes exploration the model could have
recovered from.
\end{quote}

Table~\ref{tab:ktool} ablates the prescribed-knowledge layer on AutoDS-Tools over
MLAgentBench: both branches run the identical six-agent architecture and differ
only in the instruction file. The layer arrives as one block here, so the table
isolates the layer rather than \Ktool{} alone (Section~\ref{sec:axis}); we split
it into halves on TabReD in Section~\ref{sec:grid}.

\input{tables/ktool}

Measured three times per cell, the effect separates the tasks into two groups of
very unequal weight. The costly side is unambiguous: on \texttt{clrs} the
untreated branch scores $0.3909$ pointer accuracy over three attempts while the
treated branch submits nothing in three, every attempt exhausting the task's own
agent budget; on \texttt{fathomnet} the same, $0.1435$ against nothing; on
\texttt{feedback}, MCRMSE $0.8261$ with a best attempt of $0.5403$, again against
nothing. Three tasks of eight lose their entire result to the prescription rather
than a fraction of it.

The paying side is thinner than the shape of the split suggests. Only
\texttt{ogbn-arxiv} gains by a margin clearing the spread of either branch,
$0.3866$ to $0.5069$. \texttt{identify-contrails} moves the same way, $0.0022$ to
$0.0478$, but both figures sit near zero on a metric whose useful range does not,
so the normalised $+0.96$ in Table~\ref{tab:ktool} is an artefact of dividing by
a small number and we do not read it as a result.
\texttt{spaceship-titanic} and \texttt{house-price} are level within their own
spreads, and \texttt{amp-parkinsons} moves against the prescription, SMAPE
$62.28$ without it against $86.46$ with it. Prescription is an override; here its
cost is large, repeatable and on three tasks total, while its benefit is
defensible on one of eight. This benchmark demonstrates the costly half of the
mechanism. FEDOT.LLM is the limiting case, permanently in override mode, which is
consistent with its position at the bottom of Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}.

\subsection{Splitting the layer}
\label{sec:grid}

On TabReD the two halves are separately switchable. Table~\ref{tab:grid} crosses
them: four cells, twenty-four trials each, one image, one concurrency setting,
one machine, differing in the instruction file and nothing else.

\input{tables/grid}

Both halves together beat the untreated cell on all eight tasks, so on this
family the layer does no harm. That is also the last thing to survive
aggregation. Averaged over the eight tasks the library half is worth $+0.69\%$,
the discipline half $+0.12\%$, and their interaction $-0.09\%$: one intervention
and one piece of ballast.
\begin{sloppypar}
Two tasks carry almost all of it, \texttt{ecom-offers} moving $+3.68\%$ under the
library half alone and \texttt{homecredit-default} $+0.79\%$, while none of the
other six moves by more than $0.64\%$ and five of them stay under half a
percent in either direction---at or below what the same cell reproduces across
its own three attempts.
\end{sloppypar}

The cost columns do not run parallel. Prescribing a library shortens the agent's
search and lengthens its fitting: the median trial goes from $15.4$ minutes
untreated to $30.4$ with the library named, while the bill falls from $0.0086$ to
$0.0074$ dollars. Prescribing discipline does the opposite, adding $11\%$ to the
bill and cutting the median trial to $9.7$ minutes. Which of these is cheap
depends on which budget is scarce, and Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} has already
shown that here it is the clock.

\subsection{What the unprescribed system chooses on its own}
\label{sec:spontaneous}

If prescription pays only where the model's default is wrong, then on a family
where the default is already right it should be worth close to nothing. The
terminal traces of the open harness test this without an ablation. Its
instruction names no library---task, file layout, output format, train and
validate any model of your choosing---and it fitted a gradient-boosting model on
every task in every attempt: LightGBM in twenty-three of the twenty-four trials
of Section~\ref{sec:matched} and XGBoost in the remaining one, and on the stock
image it installed LightGBM itself on six of the eight tasks. That is the family
the tool prescription would have named, so on tabular data the prescription is
not an override but a restatement of the default.

The same traces explain the ceiling. The configuration is hand-written and
near-identical across tasks and attempts: $1000$ estimators in all twenty-four
trials, learning rate $0.05$ in twenty-three, early stopping on a holdout in
twenty-three, $31$ leaves in the nineteen that set a leaf count. No search
appears in any trace---no Optuna, no grid, no randomised search, no
cross-validated sweep. The gap to the published gradient-boosting rows is
therefore not a gap in tool selection, which the harness gets right unaided, but
a gap in search: the baselines are Optuna-tuned over fifteen seeds, the agent
writes one plausible configuration and stops. Telling a weak model \emph{which}
tool to use is worth a great deal where it would otherwise choose badly and
nothing on tabular data; what is left unaddressed there---search over the chosen
tool---is what no system in our comparison performs. The per-task extraction is
released with the paper.

\subsection{And which parts of the discipline it skips}
\label{sec:discipline}

Table~\ref{tab:grid} says the discipline half is worth nothing on the score. It
does not say the half does nothing. Read for behaviour instead of accuracy, the
same four cells show a treatment landing exactly as written. Three clauses---%
compare against a trivial predictor, allocate the time budget before starting,
keep the best variant by validation rather than the last trained---leave a
signature in no trial of either cell where the discipline half is off and in
every trial of both cells where it is on. That is as clean a manipulation check
as this measurement allows.

Two further clauses separate nothing, and both show why single-arm readings
mislead. The submission is inspected in twenty-two of twenty-four trials with no
prescription at all: the multi-agent system does this unbidden, and contrasting
it with the open harness, which leaves no trace of that clause in any of its
twenty-four trials, would credit the text with a habit belonging to the
architecture. Early stopping runs the other way, universal without the library
and invisible with it, because the prescribed library performs it internally and
does not report it. A signature is evidence that a behaviour reached the trace,
not proof that it happened.

So the discipline block moves three behaviours from never to always and moves the
score by $0.12\%$. Prescribed method is not inert as instruction; it is inert as
intervention, on a family where the agent's untutored defaults were already
adequate. One connection survives with its attribution corrected: the clause the
open harness never leaves a trace of is the instruction to inspect the submission
before finishing, and the failure recorded in Section~\ref{sec:threats}---that
harness submitting one constant label for every row of a $25{,}000$-row test set
for exactly $0.5000$---is the failure that clause exists to catch. An observed
failure and a measured omission are the same fact. Supplying the clause is not
sufficient to make it pay: on TabReD the multi-agent system inspects its
submission in every trial and scores no better for it, because there was no
degenerate submission to catch.

\subsection{The same layer in another architecture}
\label{sec:kdiscterm}

Whether either half is worth anything in general is a different question, and the
two systems are far enough apart to answer it. The layer is composed on the host
and reaches only its own agent, so the only way to hand the same text to the open
harness is to put it in the task: we appended the byte-identical halves to each
instruction file and ran twenty-four trials per cell on one machine, one image,
one backbone, one concurrency setting. Every manipulation check passes---the half
that should be present appears in twenty-four trajectories of twenty-four, the
half that should be absent in none.

\input{tables/kdiscterm}

Table~\ref{tab:kdiscterm} gives the four crossed cells and a fifth, trimmed control. Accuracy is not where the answer
is. In the discipline cell every value lands
inside the untreated arm's own spread, two reproduce an untreated value to every
digit, and the mean over the seven tasks that return a number is $-0.05\%$, an
order of magnitude inside this system's $0.94\%$ noise floor.

What moves is whether a trial produces an answer at all. Trials ending without a
submission go from two of twenty-four untreated to thirteen with the discipline
half and nineteen of twenty-two with the library half, the latter at
$p=9.6\times10^{-8}$. Not one is a timeout: every one ends with the agent calling
the tool that declares the task finished, which is also how both untreated losses
end. The layer introduces no new failure mode; it makes the existing one common.

The failure is one of patience rather than method. The agent launches a fit in
the foreground, sees a terminal that has stopped producing output because its own
job still holds it, concludes that the reporting is broken rather than that the
work is unfinished, and marks the task complete---saying so in as many words in
five of the thirteen discipline-cell losses. The trials that survive the library
half are not better reasoners but more stubborn: $228$, $267$ and $355$ agent
steps over $46$ to $57$ minutes, against fourteen steps and ten minutes for the
median trial in that cell.

Two of the block's seven sections cause this, and deleting them checks it.
\emph{Training discipline} offers a hundred epochs as a sane starting budget and
tells the agent to raise it and train again rather than stop; \emph{Time budget
management} instructs it to assume it has ``hours, not minutes'' and warns that a
training phase finishing ``within a couple of minutes'' signals undertraining.
The untreated median trial here is two and a half minutes and is not
undertrained: these are gradient-boosted trees on tabular data, and the block was
written for convolutional and encoder models. Deleting those two sections and
keeping the other five restores everything. Not one trial is lost of twenty-four,
against thirteen with the full block ($p=2.6\times10^{-5}$) and two with no block
at all, a difference from the untreated cell that is not detectable ($p=0.49$).
The median trial returns to seven steps and three minutes, the bill to less than
a sixth, and the whole sweep from one hour forty-six to twenty-six minutes.

On the library side the same assumption arrives as a fact rather than an
instruction---the prescribed library is simply slow---which produces the sharpest
cell in the design and its strangest pairing. The library half alone loses
nineteen trials of twenty-two; adding the discipline half, which alone costs
thirteen of twenty-four, brings the loss down to eleven of twenty-four
($p=0.0055$). The instruction that harms by itself protects in combination, and
the mechanism is legible: what the discipline half tells this harness is that a
long-running job is normal, which is exactly what the library half requires it to
believe.

The multi-agent system met none of this: the same four cells inside its own
architecture lost respectively none, none, none and one trial of twenty-four
(Section~\ref{sec:grid}). Whatever absorbs the instruction there---a fixed
workflow, a repair loop, a planner that owns the schedule---is what makes the
prescription survivable, and none of it is part of the prescription. This is the
sharper form of the paper's thesis. Measured inside the system it ships with, the
layer is worth $+0.72\%$ and costs nothing; the same bytes handed to a different
architecture leave the answers untouched and cost most of them. A layer that has
only ever been measured in one architecture has not been measured. The practical
consequence is smaller than that framing suggests: on the discipline side the
text was two paragraphs away from being portable, and finding which two took one
cell of twenty-four trials costing eleven cents. What does not port is not the
knowledge but the assumption smuggled in beside it.

\paragraph{Two cautions.} Where the library cells do return a number it is
better, $+1.21\%$ and $+1.13\%$ against the untreated cell, in the same direction
and of the same order as the $+0.69\%$ the library half is worth inside the
multi-agent system; but those figures average over the minority of trials that
survived, and survival in those cells is exactly persistence, so we report the
direction and suppress any stronger reading. Separately, several cells reproduce
another system's value to sixteen digits. That is not two architectures
converging under a shared prescription: \texttt{sberbank-housing} returns
$0.2516404543658208$ in all three untreated multi-agent attempts and two of three
untreated open-harness attempts, with no prescription anywhere. These tasks are
deterministic given a library and its defaults, so any two runs reaching the same
call reach the same number, and the prescription changes only which call that is;
the treated cells agree with each other on $0.2514574079401016$. Repeated
attempts agreeing to every digit are therefore not independent samples of an
agent, and the noise floors of Section~\ref{sec:design} measure library
determinism as much as agent stability.

\subsection{What a different backbone changes}
\label{sec:modelaxis}

Table~\ref{tab:modelaxis} varies the model: one smaller of the same generation
and one stronger from another family, under both systems, with tasks, image,
concurrency and machine unchanged.

\input{tables/axis}

Accuracy barely moves. Median change against each system's own reference row is
$-0.06\%$ and $-0.54\%$ for the open harness and $-0.09\%$ and $-0.02\%$ for the
multi-agent system, against a bill varying elevenfold across the same rows. On
\texttt{cooking-time} two different backbones under the open harness produced
predictions identical to sixteen digits while deliberating differently: one tried
XGBoost along the way, the other went straight to LightGBM, and both ended at the
same call with the same defaults. The backbone changes the deliberation, not the
artefact.

On the normalised scale of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} the whole backbone axis
spans $0.24$ under the open harness and $0.04$ under the multi-agent system,
against the $0.49$ separating the three architectures at a fixed backbone once
prescription is taken out, or $0.59$ with the systems as they ship. The larger of
the two is not a broad effect either: of that $0.24$, $0.30$ comes from
\texttt{sberbank-housing} alone and the remaining seven tasks move the mean the
other way by $+0.07$. On the architecture-only figure, architecture is the larger
term by a factor of two against the worse-behaved backbone axis and twelve
against the other.

Where accuracy does move it moves both ways, on the hinge this section is about:
a stronger backbone exercises more discretion, and discretion departs from the
gradient-boosting default. On \texttt{ecom-offers} that departure produced the
best ROC-AUC anywhere in this paper, $0.627$ against $0.554$--$0.569$ for the
reference model; on \texttt{sberbank-housing} the same departure to a random
forest cost $46\%$ of RMSE. Prescription and backbone are different levers on one
hinge: whether the default being overridden was right to begin with.

Reliability separates the two systems, in opposite directions. Under the open
harness, trials returning no metric fall from six of twenty-four to two to none
as the backbone strengthens. Under the multi-agent system they are lowest at the
model the system was built against and rise on both sides, from one of
twenty-four at the reference to six of twenty-four below it and one of the eight
trials we could afford above it, with trials cut at the time limit moving the
same way: seventeen per cent at the reference, twenty-nine below, fifty above.
The upper point rests on those eight trials and carries a direction rather than
a rate. A
stronger model does not make that architecture finish sooner; it gives the six
agents more to say inside a fixed hour. When the binding constraint is the clock,
a better backbone buys nothing the clock will let the system deliver.

Two cautions. The loss rate is partly a property of the deployment: the same
reference model lost two trials of twenty-four here and none in the matched arm
of Section~\ref{sec:matched}, which ran on a different machine at half the
concurrency, so only the within-deployment comparison is safe and that is what
the table reports. And the axis has a floor we found by hitting it. Two models
one generation older returned nothing usable: the smaller emitted tool calls with
empty argument objects, the larger wrote a working pipeline but left its
submission in the wrong directory. Neither is a shortfall in accuracy; both are a
failure to produce a measurement. Below some level of capability the question
this axis asks stops having an answer, and that level is above what a parameter
count would suggest.

\subsection{Execution friction as a mediator}

AutoDS-Tools contains a repair loop, invoked when execution fails. It is part of
the system and active in both branches, but it does not fire equally often. The
count below is the one part of this section still taken from the earlier pair of
jobs---eighteen tasks across the two benchmarks at one attempt per cell, on the
earlier data preparation---because the repair loop was not instrumented in the
repeats; we report it as a direction and not as an effect size. Across those 36
runs it was invoked 18 times with tool prescription against 12
without, in 9 of 18 runs against 8 of 18. The imbalance concentrates where the
prescription pushes hardest---4 invocations against 0 on \texttt{clrs} and 3
against 0 on \texttt{amp-parkinsons}, both cases where the agent is steered to a
specialised library whose API it then fails on---with the reverse occurring at
smaller magnitude, 0 against 2 on \texttt{spaceship-titanic} and 3 against 4 on
\texttt{imdb}.

Read as a mediator rather than noise, this quantifies the cost side. Prescription
raises execution friction, and its net effect is positive only where the
prescribed tool is enough better to repay it. It also means the effect we measure
is that of prescription \emph{as delivered by a system able to recover from the
errors prescription causes}; in a harness without a repair loop the losses would
plausibly be larger. This also explains the disagreement in the literature noted
in Section~\ref{sec:related}: if ``knowledge injection'' bundles an override that
helps with one that hurts, and the mix of task families differs between studies,
aggregate ablations will report inconsistent and sometimes null effects for
reasons unrelated to the systems compared.

\paragraph{What the losing tasks were told, and what they did.} The trial
artefacts name the clause responsible. The discipline block instructs the agent
not to stop after ``an arbitrary small budget (5 or 15 epochs is almost always
badly undertrained)'' and offers ``$\sim 100$ epochs'' as a starting point. On
\texttt{clrs} the untreated branch chose $5$, $10$, $20$ and $30$ epochs over its
run---exactly the range the clause forbids---and reached $0.3909$ pointer
accuracy, taking $92$ minutes of a $120$-minute budget on one attempt and running
the budget out entirely on the other two while still submitting, at $0.4118$ and
$0.4404$. The treated branch submitted nothing in three attempts. A branch that
already consumes most of the budget at $5$ to $30$ epochs cannot also honour a
prescription calling those figures undertrained and naming a hundred. The
instruction is not wrong as advice; it is written without reference to the budget
the task grants, and on this task the two are incompatible. We cannot read the
treated traces directly---the agent is killed at the ceiling before it writes
one, itself a small piece of evidence---so what we report is the untreated
configuration, the clause, and the outcome.
