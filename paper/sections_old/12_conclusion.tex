\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We isolated the architectural axis of agentic AutoML by holding the backbone
model fixed and varying only the surrounding system, and we measured the result
against reference methods that were tuned.

Architecture is worth a great deal and not quite enough. Under an identical open
31B model, the systems span $0.26$ to $0.85$ of the range defined by eighteen
expert-tuned baselines, while tuned XGBoost sits at $0.87$; the strongest of
them is level with tuned LightGBM and ahead of tuned CatBoost.
The gap architecture closes is real and large; the one it leaves open has
narrowed to $0.02$ but has not closed.

The relationship is not monotonic in architectural weight, in either direction.
The most constrained system ranks near the bottom and the highest-ranking of the
three is neither extreme, which suggests the useful question is not how much
structure a system imposes but which decision that structure removes. Our
evidence is that constraint pays where it overrides a default the weak model gets
wrong and costs where the default was already right, visible in an ablation whose
sign flips between task families. The two directions are not balanced: at three
attempts per cell the costly one is total on three tasks of eight while the
paying one rests on a single task. What we can assert is the cost of override;
the benefit is the weaker half of the claim, and one that plausibly explains why
aggregate knowledge and memory ablations in the literature disagree.

For practitioners the implication is narrow. On established families with strong
classical solutions a tuned baseline remains the correct choice, and an agentic
system's value lies in coverage, cost, and reaching tasks where no such baseline
exists---as the case studies show, where two of three published author baselines
were exceeded by an open $31$B model told which library and featurisation to use.
What the practitioner supplies, and whether the system would have found it alone,
is the variable to watch.

\section*{Data availability}

\begin{sloppypar}
All tasks, both instruction variants and every execution trace behind these
numbers are public. The benchmark adaptations and the two instruction files per
task are on HuggingFace as \texttt{danil-e/harbor-datasets-tabred} and
\texttt{danil-e/harbor-datasets-mlab}; the tabular adapter is
\texttt{AaLexUser/harbor-tabred-adapter}. Trajectories, submissions, scorer
outputs and per-job telemetry for the earlier runs are on the Harbor hub under
job identifiers \texttt{7593cc7b} and \texttt{1a42d1fe} (Terminus-2 on TabReD and
on MLAgentBench) and \texttt{3113aba7} and \texttt{56768b37} (the two
AutoDS-Tools branches); any trial can be retrieved with \texttt{harbor hub trial
download}, and the values in our tables were checked against those files task by
task. The repeats reported here ran on our own hardware and are released with the
data package under the job names \texttt{autods-mlab-nolayer-full},
\texttt{autods-mlab-c1-full}, \texttt{autods-mlab-layernow} and
\texttt{terminus-mlab-full}. The package also carries the consolidated metric
table across all systems and benchmarks, the measured official split sizes for
the eight TabReD tasks, the per-task tables this paper reports in summary, the
learner and hyperparameter extraction of Section~\ref{sec:spontaneous}, the job
telemetry of Table~\ref{tab:cost}, the fingerprints of both editions of the
prescribed-knowledge layer with the script that recomputes them, and the scripts
that turn all of it into every table printed here.
\end{sloppypar}
