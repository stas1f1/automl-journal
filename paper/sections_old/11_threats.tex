\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Comparability of data slices and provisioning.} Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}
is restricted to systems run through the adapter that uses the full official
temporal splits and the benchmark's own metrics in raw target units. Earlier
AutoDS-Tools runs used a different preparation: a 40{,}000-row training slice
spanning the official train and validation splits, a test slice covering
$16.7\%$--$61.4\%$ of the official test window in every case its final rows, and
an extra row-order column the official protocol withholds. Measured split sizes
for all eight tasks are released; those runs are excluded here and the
leaderboard uses a re-run of the same system on the common adapter, so the
difference between the two sets can be read as the price of the preparation. The
earlier comparison also differed in what was installed, the AutoDS-Tools arm
carrying the libraries its prescription names while the others used the stock
image. Section~\ref{sec:matched} closes that axis directly---offered the enriched
image, the open harness scores inside its own noise floor on seven of eight tasks
and never names a library the image adds---so the threat stands only for the
FEDOT.LLM runs, which we could not repeat.

\paragraph{Our own provisioning was broken, and the systems repaired it.} The
enriched image brings LightGBM as a dependency, but the base image ships no
OpenMP runtime and the wheel carries none. The import succeeds and the failure
arrives inside the fit, as a broken worker pool that reads as a modelling error
and is not one. Forty-five trials met it---twenty-two of twenty-four
AutoDS-Tools trials and twenty-three of twenty-four matched-provisioning
trials---and all forty-five diagnosed and repaired it in a single step, which is
why it went unnoticed until we read the traces. Repeating the whole arm on a
corrected image agrees to within $0.13\%$ on seven tasks and $0.34\%$ on the
eighth, so the defect cost no measurable accuracy. It could still have distorted
a crossed design, since the fault sits inside the prescribed library and can
strike only the cells where that prescription is on. Every cell we report ran on
the corrected image.

\paragraph{Repeats and numeric precision.} Published baselines are means over
fifteen seeds; our agent rows are one run for FEDOT.LLM and the mean of three
attempts otherwise. The two agentic systems do not share a noise floor:
Terminus-2 reaches a relative standard deviation of $0.94\%$ while AutoDS-Tools
reproduces itself to within $0.07\%$, so the $1\%$ threshold used across systems
is set by the noisier of the pair and would be far too coarse for comparing
configurations of one system, where a tenth of a percent is already outside the
spread. FEDOT.LLM metrics reached us at two decimal places. Sweeping every task
independently to both ends of its rounding interval moves its average rank
between $16.6$ and $20.3$ of $21$ and its normalised mean between $0.05$ and
$0.47$; no claim here depends on any part of that interval, but its individual
rank cells should be read as indicative.

\begin{sloppypar}
\paragraph{Scorers that accept degenerate submissions.} The prohibition on
constant submissions belongs to the prescribed-knowledge layer, not to any task:
fingerprinting the layer shipped with each of the ten MLAgentBench tasks shows it
present in all ten, byte-identical within a family. It therefore reached the
treated branch of AutoDS-Tools everywhere and reached neither the untreated
branch nor Terminus-2, whose harness receives the task file alone. The asymmetry
is between arms rather than tasks and, since the clause constrains rather than
assists, it runs against the only arm that carried it. Both degenerate
submissions we found belong to Terminus-2. On \texttt{imdb} it submitted one
label for all $25{,}000$ test rows and the scorer returned an accuracy of exactly
$0.5000$, recording a submission with no model in it at chance level rather than
rejecting it; on \texttt{amp-parkinsons} $344$ of $559$ rows ($61.5\%$) carry the
same global value in all four targets. We exclude the \texttt{imdb} figure and
read \texttt{amp-parkinsons} as a partial fallback. The wider point belongs to
the benchmark: on eight of the ten tasks nothing in the scoring path separates a
trained model from a constant, so an agent that fails silently is scored as
though it had merely performed poorly.
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{An active repair loop inside the treated system.} The repair loop of
AutoDS-Tools is architecture rather than prescription, but it is invoked
asymmetrically between the branches (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}) and therefore
partially masks the cost of prescription. We report invocation counts alongside
the scores rather than adjusting for them. Disabling it in both branches would
measure the interaction between prescription and repair, a different quantity
which we did not run and which is the experiment we would run next: the repair
loop is the most plausible explanation for why prescription is survivable in this
architecture and not in the other (Section~\ref{sec:kdiscterm}).

\begin{sloppypar}
\paragraph{Trials lost to the wall-clock ceiling.} Four of the twenty-four
AutoDS-Tools trials on the common adapter reached the $3600$\,s limit; three had
already written a submission and are scored like any other, and the fourth had
not and is excluded, so \texttt{homesite-insurance} rests on two attempts.
Scoring the lost trial as zero was considered and rejected: a timeout measures
the budget, not the model, and averaging one in would move
\texttt{homesite-insurance} from $0.961$ to $0.641$, last of the twenty-one
methods on a task where the system is otherwise near the top, and would carry
the normalised mean from $0.85$ to $-0.33$---below every published baseline,
including linear regression---while the average rank moved only from $6.6$ to
$8.8$. The two scales disagree because the normalisation has no floor, which is
the second reason not to score a timeout at all. The exclusion is not neutral either, since it drops the
slowest attempts and leaves the means mildly optimistic about a system whose
failures correlate with the tasks it finds hardest.
\end{sloppypar}

\paragraph{Data and validation access.} Several MLAgentBench tasks use subsets of
the original data and none uses an official competition test split, so absolute
values there are not comparable with public leaderboards and every comparison in
this paper is internal by construction. On TabReD the agents receive a labelled
validation split whereas the published baselines train on the training split
alone---a small advantage to the agents, working against the direction of our
main conclusion.

\paragraph{The prescription is a moving target.} The layer is composed on the
host by the agent package installed when the runner was set up, not by the copy
pip places inside the container, so reinstalling from a newer checkout changes
the treatment without changing anything visible in the run configuration. We
fixed the version by measurement rather than by tag, and the two benchmarks turn
out to have run against two editions. Every TabReD run applied the layer composed
by the branch we cloned: $4972$ characters for tabular tasks, SHA-256 prefix
\texttt{a58b4d40678ff1aa}, verified byte-identical to the tail of
\texttt{agent/instruction.md} in each trial. The MLAgentBench runs applied the
edition shipped with the tasks: $5085$ characters for tabular
(\texttt{bf1e4e7c8c4960a2}), $4363$ for vision, $4163$ for text, $4153$ for
graph. The two carry the same eight section headings and differ by $113$
characters in the shared discipline block and $668$ in the graph library block.
The difference is not cosmetic: the cloned edition advises that SMAPE penalises a
non-zero prediction against a zero target maximally and tells graph agents to
mask unreachable candidates before the softmax---two hints written for particular
tasks---where the shipped edition carries the general prohibition on constant
submissions instead. The repository recomputes every fingerprint above.

We ran the two editions against each other rather than leaving the threat at a
character count: same system, same eight tasks, same images, verifier and
concurrency, three attempts each. Under the shipped edition the treated branch
produces no submission at all on \texttt{feedback}, \texttt{clrs} and
\texttt{fathomnet}, nine attempts of nine; under the edition composed today it
submits on all three, at MCRMSE $0.5512$, pointer accuracy $0.1281$ and micro-F1
$0.1507$. Where the budget was never binding the shipped edition is the better of
the two---SMAPE $86.46$ against $93.15$, accuracy $0.5069$ against
$0.4683$---and the remaining pair is level. Counting submissions rather than
scores, the shipped edition yields $15$ of $24$ and the composed edition $21$ of
$24$. The edit is thus not an improvement or a regression but a move along the
axis this paper is about: one edition presses harder on training discipline and
pays in unfinished runs, the other presses less and finishes. On three of eight
tasks the gap between two editions of one prescription exceeds the gap between
having the prescription and not having it. A result of this kind cannot be
repeated from a description of the layer, only from its bytes, and the bytes are
what is least often reported.

\paragraph{Single backbone family.} The comparison uses one model throughout.
Section~\ref{sec:modelaxis} varies it under both systems and finds the accuracy
ordering unchanged, so the ranking is not a backbone artefact; what the backbone
changes is reliability and cost, and in opposite directions for the two
architectures. That rests on three models, two from one vendor, and one task
family. A stronger backbone that reasons less verbosely might not push the
multi-agent system past the time limit at all, and we cannot separate capability
from verbosity with what we ran.
