Large language models have made automated data science agents practical, but the
field still lacks a measurement of what the surrounding architecture is worth.
Reported gains confound the choice of backbone model with the choice of harness,
and agentic systems are seldom compared against baselines a competent
practitioner would tune. We hold the model fixed at a single open 31B-parameter
model and vary the architecture across three points of the design space: a rigid
pipeline that configures a fixed AutoML backend, a six-agent system, and an open
agentic harness. All run inside one execution environment on the official
temporal splits of a real-world tabular benchmark, against eighteen expert-tuned
reference methods.

Architecture is worth about half of the achievable range. Normalised against
those baselines, the systems span $0.26$ to $0.85$ as they ship and $0.26$ to
$0.75$ once the prescribed-knowledge layer is removed from the one that carries
it. The strongest is level with tuned LightGBM at $0.85$ and ahead of tuned
CatBoost at $0.80$, but does not reach tuned XGBoost at $0.87$. The relationship
is not monotonic in architectural weight: the rigid pipeline ranks last of the
three and the multi-agent system first, with the open harness between them, and
most of the margin between the two agentic systems is prescription rather than
architecture.

The value of that prescription changes sign with the starting point. It helps
where a weak model would otherwise reach for an unsuitable primitive and hurts
where a working baseline already encodes the right choice, and the two directions
are not of comparable weight: at three attempts per cell the cost is total on
three tasks of eight, the treated branch submitting nothing at all, while a
defensible benefit appears on one. We further show that one ``knowledge
injection'' intervention is at least two---which tool to reach for, and how to
train and validate---that the tool half is the smaller part of the layer it
names, and that the unprescribed system selects the prescribed family of tool
unaided on every tabular task. Together these account for why aggregate
ablations of knowledge injection have reported inconsistent effects.
