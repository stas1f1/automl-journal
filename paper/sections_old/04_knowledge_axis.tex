\section{The prescribed-knowledge axis}
\label{sec:axis}

Interventions that supply a system with knowledge it would otherwise have to
derive are usually treated as one thing. In our material they are two, they are
of very unequal size, and they do not add.

\begin{description}
  \item[\Ktool{} --- tool prescription.] Which class of instrument to reach for:
        a dedicated tabular AutoML library for tabular data, a vision backbone
        library for images, a graph learning library for graphs, a transformer
        library for text. It removes the question ``what does one normally use
        for this?''
  \item[\Kdisc{} --- training discipline.] How to train and how to check
        oneself: the validation protocol, the handling of temporal shift,
        leakage checks, and the prohibition of degenerate submissions. It is
        orthogonal to the choice of library.
\end{description}

Separating them is not a matter of tidiness: crossed properly, the two halves do
not add. On the normalised scale of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} the tool half
alone reaches $0.87$, the discipline half alone $0.77$, and the two together
$0.85$---the stronger half does better on its own than in company. Averaging
per-task relative change instead puts the pair a hair ahead, $+0.72\%$ against
$+0.69\%$, so which of the two is nominally better depends on the aggregation;
what does not depend on it is that the pair is worth far less than the sum of
its halves, $+0.72\%$ against $+0.81\%$. The
interaction is sharper still on the second architecture, where the library block
alone loses $19$ of $22$ trials to the time limit while adding the discipline
block on top brings that to $11$ of $24$: there the half that harms alone
protects in combination (Section~\ref{sec:kdiscterm}).

\texttt{amp-parkinsons} is a cautionary case about doing this by inference
instead. An earlier draft attributed a degradation there to a prohibition of
constant submissions said to have been added for that task alone. Fingerprinting
the layer text shipped with each of the ten MLAgentBench tasks shows otherwise:
the prohibition belongs to the discipline block and reaches every task of a
family identically, so what differs is not the task but the edition of the layer
(Section~\ref{sec:threats}). The figure that draft compared against came from
the other edition and cannot be re-read from any artefact, so we drop it.
Repeating both branches three times gives numbers that can: the layer as a whole
moves \texttt{amp-parkinsons} from a mean SMAPE of $62.28$ without it to $86.46$
with it, and each branch is tight enough about its own mean---$58.21$ to $66.35$
without, $81.78$ to $90.08$ with---that the direction is not in doubt.
While the two halves are delivered as a single instruction layer, no row of the
ablation can be attributed to either, and the tables report the layer as a
whole.

\subsection{The layer is named after its smaller part}

In the implementation we study the layer is composed literally as
$\texttt{instruction} + \texttt{LIBRARY}[\textit{family}] + \texttt{DISCIPLINE}$,
and the first section of \texttt{DISCIPLINE} is a \textsc{hardware} block that
prescribes neither a tool nor a training protocol. That makes the split
measurable rather than conceptual. Reading the character counts from the module
that composed the layer, and checking them against the instruction each container
received: the \textsc{library} block runs from 369 characters for text to 1291
for tabular tasks, the \textsc{hardware} block is a fixed 668 and the
\textsc{discipline} block a fixed 3008. The tool prescription---the intervention the layer is named after---accounts for
between $9.1\%$ and $26.0\%$ of the text. The \textsc{hardware} block, pinning
down how the device is resolved before training, is neither tool prescription nor
training discipline and forms a third category; the \textsc{discipline} block is
the largest component on every task. On the tabular tasks the whole layer runs to 4972 characters against a task
statement of 1050, close to five times the length of the problem it is attached
to. This matters for attribution: any effect ascribed to tool prescription in an
aggregate ablation is at least as likely to originate in the two fixed blocks
travelling with it, and those are constant across modalities while the reported
effects are not.

\subsection{A prescription presupposes its environment}

Tool prescription is realisable only where the prescribed tool exists, which
makes it a property of the environment as much as of the text. The layer states
that the specialized library is ``pre-installed'' and forbids falling back on
general-purpose estimators, so it presumes an image provisioned accordingly.
Against an image without that library it does not produce a weaker treatment but
a failure. We met this directly: the benchmark's own image ships \texttt{numpy},
\texttt{pandas} and \texttt{scikit-learn} on Python~3.13, on which the prescribed
tabular library does not build, so an agent given the prescription there reads
the data, writes a correct pipeline, and fails at import, scoring zero while
having done nothing wrong. The environment is therefore part of the treatment and
we report the provisioning of each arm explicitly
(Section~\ref{sec:design}). This is also a caution for the literature: an
ablation of ``prescribe a specialized library'' measures the prescription only
where the provisioning already holds, and otherwise measures the provisioning.

\subsection{Where each system sits}

Table~\ref{tab:designpoints} places the three systems on this axis.

FEDOT.LLM is not missing \Ktool{} but has it welded into its architecture: the
instruction ``solve this through FEDOT'' \emph{is} the pipeline. That is the
extreme point of the design rather than a hole in it, and it is what makes
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} interesting---the system whose tool prescription is
maximal and non-removable performs worst on exactly the family the prescribed
tool was built for. \Kdisc{} is the only half that could in principle be crossed
with every system, though we cross it on the two agentic ones only;
\Ktool{} is crossed on AutoDS-Tools and Terminus-2, with FEDOT.LLM entering as
the reference point at which it cannot be switched off.
