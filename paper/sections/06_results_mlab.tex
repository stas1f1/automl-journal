Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} ranks AutoDS-Tools first on TabReD, with FEDOT.LLM
and Terminus-2 level behind it, and traces most of the lead to the
instruction file. We propose a sharper statement, which this section tests: constraint pays where
it removes a decision the weak model reliably gets wrong, and everywhere else
it removes exploration the model could have recovered from. MLAgentBench
supplies a working training script in every task, so a default is in place
before the agent starts; this is the second starting point of the design, and
the section answers RQ3 under it.

\subsection{Where a working script is supplied, the layer costs whole tasks}
\label{sec:signflip}

The two AutoDS-Tools column groups of Table~\ref{tab:mlab} ablate the
prescribed-knowledge layer over MLAgentBench. Both branches run the identical six-agent architecture on
identical images and differ only in the instruction file. The layer arrives as
one block here, so the table isolates the whole layer; the halves are
separated on TabReD in Section~\ref{sec:grid}.


Measured three times per cell, the effect splits the tasks into two groups of
very unequal weight (Table~\ref{tab:mlab}). The costly side is
unambiguous. On \texttt{clrs} the untreated branch scores $\mlabClrsOff$
pointer accuracy over three attempts, while the treated branch submits nothing
in three, every attempt exhausting the task's agent budget. On
\texttt{fathomnet} the untreated branch scores $\mlabFathomnetOff$ against
nothing. On \texttt{feedback} it scores MCRMSE $\mlabFeedbackOff$ with a best
attempt of $\mlabFeedbackOffLo$, again against nothing. On \mlabWiped{} tasks of \mlabRepeated{} the treated branch submits nothing in any
attempt.

The paying side is thinner than the shape of the split suggests. Only
\texttt{ogbn-arxiv} gains by a margin that clears the spread of either branch,
$\mlabOgbnOff$ to $\mlabOgbnOn$. \texttt{identify-contrails} moves the same
way, $\mlabContrailsOff$ to $\mlabContrailsOn$, but both figures sit near zero
on a metric whose useful range does not, so the $+0.96$ in the table divides
one small number by another and we do not read it as a result.
\texttt{spaceship-titanic} and \texttt{house-price} are level within their own
spreads, and \texttt{amp-parkinsons} moves against the prescription, SMAPE
$\mlabAmpOff$ without it against $\mlabAmpOn$ with it. The prescription
replaces a decision that the supplied script had already made; on
MLAgentBench that replacement leaves no submission on \mlabWiped{} of
\mlabRepeated{} tasks in every attempt and improves one of \mlabRepeated{} by
a defensible margin. FEDOT.LLM replaces that decision on every task by
construction, and on TabReD, where the tool it fixes is the right one, it
loses nothing by it (Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard}).

What separates this outcome from TabReD is the starting point. There the
branch carrying the prescription wins all eight tasks at three attempts per
cell (Table~\ref{tab:grid}, column \emph{both}); here the branch without it
wins \mlabOffWins{} of the \mlabRepeated{} tasks we could repeat, and
\mlabWiped{} of those wins are total. Both counts come from the same system,
backbone and pair of instruction files. Where nothing is supplied, a
prescription that points the model at a reasonable tool improves on what it
would otherwise build; where the supplied script already contains that
decision, overriding it discards work that was correct. The magnitudes are as
unequal as the signs: on MLAgentBench the prescription can turn a usable score
into no submission, while on TabReD every cell returns a score and the gain,
on the scale where 0 is the weakest and 1 the strongest published method per
task, runs from $\gridBothEffMin$ on \texttt{\gridBothEffMinTask} to
$\gridBothEffMax$ on \texttt{\gridBothEffMaxTask}, median
$\gridBothEffMedian$.

The two halves of this comparison are not a crossed design: the benchmarks
differ in tasks, metrics and time budgets as well as in starting point, so we
observe one factor across two protocols and do not add the win counts. Closing
the confound would need a greenfield variant of the MLAgentBench tasks or an
improve-a-baseline variant of the TabReD ones; we did not build one and state
the weaker claim, which holds inside each protocol separately: prescription
costs no submission where nothing was supplied, and costs every submission on
\mlabWiped{} of \mlabRepeated{} tasks where a working script was.

\paragraph{Observation 6} The layer that helps on every TabReD task leaves no
submission on \mlabWiped{} of \mlabRepeated{} MLAgentBench tasks and helps
defensibly on one; what differs between the two benchmarks is whether a working
default was already in place.

\subsection{The same layer in another architecture}
\label{sec:kdiscterm}

Whether either half of the layer is worth anything in general is a different
question, and the two agentic systems are far enough apart to ask it. The
layer is composed on the host and reaches only its own agent, so we appended
the byte-identical halves to the Terminus-2 task file and ran twenty-four
trials per cell on one machine, one image, one backbone and one concurrency
setting; the half that should be present appears in every trajectory, the
half that should be absent in none. Table~\ref{tab:kdiscterm} gives the four
crossed cells and a fifth, trimmed control.

\input{tables/kdiscterm}

Accuracy does not move: in the discipline cell every value lands inside the
untreated arm's own spread, and the mean over the \ktDiscTasks{} tasks that
return a number is $\ktDiscRel\%$, an order of magnitude inside this system's
$\noiseTerminusMax\%$ noise floor. What moves is whether a trial produces an
answer at all. Trials ending without a submission go from \ktNeitherLost{} of
twenty-four untreated to \ktDiscLost{} with the discipline half and
\ktToolLost{} of \ktToolTrials{} with the library half
($p=\ktPToolVsNeither$). None is a timeout. The agent launches a fit in the
foreground, sees a terminal that has stopped producing output because its own
job holds it, concludes that the reporting is broken and marks the task
complete; the layer makes an existing failure common.

Two of the discipline block's seven sections cause this. One offers a hundred
epochs as a sane starting budget and tells the agent to raise it; the other
tells it to assume it has ``hours, not minutes'' and that a training phase
finishing ``within a couple of minutes'' signals undertraining. The untreated
median trial here is two and a half minutes of gradient-boosted trees and is
not undertrained; the block was written for convolutional and encoder models.
Deleting those two sections and keeping the other five loses no trial of
twenty-four, against \ktDiscLost{} with the full block ($p=\ktPTrimVsDisc$)
and \ktNeitherLost{} with no block at all ($p=\ktPTrimVsNeither$, not
detectable), and returns the median trial to \ktTrimSteps{} steps and three
minutes. With the library block appended the fit is long because the
prescribed library is slow, and there the discipline block protects: the
library half alone loses \ktToolLost{} trials of \ktToolTrials{}, both halves
together \ktBothLost{} of twenty-four ($p=\ktPToolVsBoth$), because the
discipline half tells the harness that a long-running job is normal.

AutoDS-Tools met none of this: the same four cells inside its own
architecture lost none, none, none and one trial of twenty-four
(Section~\ref{sec:grid}), so the effect of a prescription has to be measured
in each architecture separately. The fix was small: deleting two of seven
sections made the block safe for Terminus-2, and finding them took one cell
of twenty-four trials.

\paragraph{Observation 7} Handed to Terminus-2, the layer leaves accuracy
inside the noise floor and costs \ktDiscLost{} of twenty-four trials with the
discipline half and \ktToolLost{} of \ktToolTrials{} with the library half;
deleting the two paragraphs about training time restores every trial.
