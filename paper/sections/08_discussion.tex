\subsection{What the ordering does and does not show}

Compared as deployed, the six-agent AutoDS-Tools ranks first on TabReD, and
the rigid pipeline FEDOT.LLM and the open harness Terminus-2 are level in
average rank, FEDOT.LLM ahead on the normalised mean through one task. We do
not claim an optimum in the middle: three points cannot fix the shape of a
curve, and most of AutoDS-Tools' lead belongs to its prescribed-knowledge
layer. With the layer removed the scores are FEDOT.LLM $\nFedot$,
AutoDS-Tools $\nAutoDSOff$ and Terminus-2 $\nTerminus$ on the normalised
scale, within $\spanArch$ of each other, of the order of their run-to-run
noise. What the data support is this: on tabular tasks the three
architectures, from the rigid pipeline to the open harness, reach the same
region once what ships with them is taken away, and what separates the
deployed systems is the instruction file of one and the backend search of
another. Whether a designed middle ground would beat all three is a
hypothesis this study cannot test.

\subsection{What the case studies add}
\label{sec:cases-discussion}

The case studies of Section~\ref{sec:cases} bear on the ceiling of
Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} and on RQ3. Two of three author baselines were
exceeded on the authors' own metric by an open 31B model working from the task
text alone; applied scientific baselines are often well below what a competent
tabular pipeline achieves, so the gap to tuned XGBoost on TabReD is a statement
about expert-tuned baselines specifically. FEDOT.LLM, level with Terminus-2
on TabReD, beat both agents on F-DATA, where the task reduces to tuning a
fixed backend. On a family where
the prescribed library is a sound choice, the prescription still cost
Terminus-2 all \caseTermPrescTotal{} attempts, for the reason
Section~\ref{sec:kdiscterm} gives, and it pays only inside an architecture
that can wait for the prescribed tool.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Six threats remain after the deviations accounted for in
Section~\ref{sec:deviations}. First, repeats. Published baselines are means
over fifteen seeds; our agent rows are means of three attempts, and on
\allIdentFedot{} of eight tasks the three FEDOT.LLM attempts agree to every
digit, so its spread is understated. Its row also reads the column types from
the task schema (Section~\ref{sec:deviations}), an input the agents take from
the task text.

Second, scorers that accept degenerate submissions. The prohibition on
constant submissions belongs to the layer, so only the treated branch of
AutoDS-Tools carried it, and any bias from the asymmetry works against that
arm. Both degenerate submissions we found belong to Terminus-2: on
\texttt{imdb} one label for all $25{,}000$ test rows, scored at exactly
$0.5000$ and excluded, and on \texttt{amp-parkinsons} one global value in
$61.5\%$ of rows, read as a partial fallback. On eight of the ten MLAgentBench
tasks nothing in the scoring path separates a trained model from a constant.

Third, the repair loop. AutoDS-Tools repairs failed executions more often with
the layer than without (Section~\ref{sec:friction}), so it partially masks the
cost of prescription. We report the counts without adjustment; disabling the
loop in both branches is the experiment we would run next.

Fourth, the lost trial: \rerunLost{} of the twenty-four AutoDS-Tools trials on
TabReD reached the one-hour limit with nothing to score and is excluded, so
\texttt{homesite-insurance} rests on two attempts. Scoring it as zero would
move that task from ROC-AUC $0.961$ to $\timeoutZeroHomesite$, the normalised
mean of AutoDS-Tools from $\nAutoDS$ to $\timeoutZeroNorm$, below every
published baseline, and its average rank only from $\rkAutoDS$ to
$\timeoutZeroRank$, since the normalisation has no floor. The exclusion is
not neutral either: it drops the slowest attempt of a system whose failures
fall on the tasks it finds hardest.

Fifth, data access. Several MLAgentBench tasks use subsets of the original
data and none an official test split, so every comparison there is internal.
On TabReD the agents receive a labelled validation split and the published
baselines train on the training split alone, a small advantage to the agents
that works against our main conclusion.

Sixth, the backbone. Section~\ref{sec:modelaxis} varies it under both agentic
systems and finds the accuracy ordering unchanged, so the ranking is not a
backbone artefact, but that rests on three models, two from one vendor, and
one task family. A stronger backbone that reasons less verbosely might not
push the multi-agent system into the time limit at all, and we cannot separate
capability from verbosity with what we ran.
