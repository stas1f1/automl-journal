LLM-based agents write and run machine-learning pipelines. What the
system around the model adds is unmeasured, and agents are seldom compared
with tuned baselines. We fix one open 31B-parameter model and run three
designs: FEDOT.LLM, a rigid pipeline over a fixed AutoML
backend; AutoDS-Tools, a six-agent system; and Terminus-2, an open harness
with a shell. On the eight tabular tasks of TabReD,
scored on a scale where 0 is the weakest and 1 the strongest of eighteen
Optuna-tuned methods, AutoDS-Tools reaches $\nAutoDS$, level with tuned
LightGBM and $\gapXGB$ short of tuned XGBoost; FEDOT.LLM reaches $\nFedot$
and Terminus-2 $\nTerminus$. Most of AutoDS-Tools' lead is its
instruction file: without it the three architectures lie within $\spanArch$
of each other. FEDOT.LLM, whose backend searches over models, beats every
published method on one task. The instruction file has two parts, one naming
the library, the other saying how to train and validate; on TabReD they
improve the task metric by $\gridToolMeanU\%$ and $\gridDiscMeanU\%$, and both
together by $\gridBothMeanU\%$. On MLAgentBench, where every task starts from a
working script, the same file leaves no submission on \mlabWiped{} of
\mlabRepeated{} tasks and helps on one. Appended to Terminus-2, the training
advice loses \ktDiscLost{} of \ktDiscTrials{} trials at unchanged accuracy;
deleting two paragraphs about training time restores them. On
three published scientific tasks Terminus-2, given the task text alone,
matched or beat the author baselines where the split is comparable, and the
text that names LightAutoML cost it all \caseTermPrescTotal{} attempts.
