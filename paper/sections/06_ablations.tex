\subsection{Harness alone: the instruction file removed}
\label{sec:abl-harness}

With the instruction file turned off, AutoDS-Tools scores $\nAutoDSOff$
on the normalised scale, between Terminus-2 ($\nTerminus$) and FEDOT.LLM
($\nFedot$). On the same image, AutoDS-Tools without the file is ahead of
Terminus-2 on \starchAutoDSvsTerminusWins{} of 8 tasks by
$\starchAutoDSvsTerminusDiff$ (interval $\starchAutoDSvsTerminusLo$ to
$\starchAutoDSvsTerminusHi$, $p=\starchAutoDSvsTerminusP$): a small and
consistent lead that does not reach significance.  FEDOT.LLM performs similarly to Terminus-2
on average ($p=\stshipFedotvsTerminusP$) with task-level differences in both directions,
from $+1.46$ on \texttt{ecom-offers} to
$-0.38$ on \texttt{cooking-time}.

\paragraph{Finding 5} Without the instruction file, the mean scores of the
three harnesses are close and no pairwise difference reaches $p<0.05$. The
harnesses still behave differently task by task: AutoDS-Tools is slightly
and consistently ahead of Terminus-2, and FEDOT.LLM varies most.

\subsection{The instruction file inside AutoDS-Tools}
\label{sec:abl-file}

Table~\ref{tab:grid} combines the two parts of the file on AutoDS-Tools: 
four cells of 24 trials, all using the same image and machine and 
differing only in the instruction file. The whole file raises
the normalised score from $\nAutoDSOff$ to $\nAutoDS$, improving all eight
tasks ($\stfileBothDiff$, interval $\stfileBothLo$ to $\stfileBothHi$,
$p=\stfileBothP$). The library instruction alone raises it to $\nGridTool$
(\stfileToolWins{} of 8 tasks, $p=\stfileToolP$), on par with tuned
XGBoost ($\sttoolVsXGBDiff$, $p=\sttoolVsXGBP$) and the best cell in the
paper. Adding the discipline instruction on top changes it by
$\stbothVsToolDiff$ ($p=\stbothVsToolP$). The discipline instruction alone
raises it to $\nGridDisc$ (\stfileDiscWins{} of 8, $p=\stfileDiscP$).
The traces show what the library instruction changes: with it,
AutoDS-Tools calls LightAutoML~\citep{vakhrushev2021lightautoml} in every
trial that left a trace (24 and 20 of 24 in the two cells), and without it
writes LightGBM, XGBoost or CatBoost
in all 24 trials of both cells (Section~\ref{sec:spontaneous}). 
In task metrics library instruction improves the
mean by $\gridToolMeanU\%$, the discipline instruction by
$\gridDiscMeanU\%$ and both together by $\gridBothMeanU\%$, below the
$\gridSumHalvesU\%$ sum of the two. Most of the library effect comes from two tasks:
\texttt{ecom-offers} ($\gridToolEcom\%$) and
\texttt{homecredit-default} ($\gridToolHomecredit\%$). No other task moves
by more than $\gridToolRestMax\%$. Of the $\gapAgentsShip$ by which
AutoDS-Tools with its file leads Terminus-2, $\layerWorthNorm$ is therefore the
file and $\gapAgentsOff$ the harness.
\input{tables/grid}

The two parts also have different costs. Naming the library in the 
instruction shortens the agent's search while lengthening the fit:
the median trial goes from
$\gridNeitherMin$ minutes without instructions to $\gridToolMin$ with the
library named, and the API cost per trial falls from $\gridNeitherCost$ to
$\gridToolCost$ dollars. The discipline instruction does the opposite,
adding $\gridDiscCostPct\%$ to the cost and cutting the median trial to
$\gridDiscMin$ minutes. No cell lost more than one trial of 24.

\paragraph{Finding 6} Inside AutoDS-Tools the instruction file improves all
eight TabReD tasks ($p=\stfileBothP$) and accounts for $\layerWorthNorm$ of
the $\gapAgentsShip$ lead over Terminus-2. The library instruction achieves
the effect by using LightAutoML for modelling, and alone reaches the level
of tuned XGBoost. The combined impact of the 
library and discipline instructions is smaller than the sum of their individual effects.

\subsection{The same file inside Terminus-2}
\label{sec:abl-terminus}

To test the file in a second harness we appended the byte-identical parts to
the Terminus-2 task statement and ran 24 trials per cell on one machine,
image and language model (Table~\ref{tab:kdiscterm}). Accuracy does not change: in
the discipline cell every value lies inside the spread of the cell without
instructions (mean difference $\sttermDiscDiff$, interval $\sttermDiscLo$ to
$\sttermDiscHi$). The percentage of trials that end without a submission
changes:
from \ktNeitherLost{} of 24 without instructions to \ktDiscLost{}
with the discipline instruction ($p=\ktPDiscVsNeither$), \ktToolLost{} of
\ktToolTrials{} with the library instruction ($p=\ktPToolVsNeither$) and
\ktBothLost{} of 24 with both. None of these trials reached the time limit.
In each case, the agent started a fit, observed that the terminal had
stopped producing output, assumed that reporting was broken, and marked the task complete.
\input{tables/kdiscterm}

Two paragraphs of the discipline instruction cause the losses in the
discipline cell. One offers a hundred epochs as a starting budget. The other
tells the agent to assume it has ``hours, not minutes'' and that a training
phase finishing ``within a couple of minutes'' signals undertraining. The
median trial without instructions is two and a half minutes of
gradient-boosted trees. Deleting the two paragraphs loses no trial of 24
($p=\ktPTrimVsDisc$ against the full instruction). With the library
instruction, the fit takes longer because LightAutoML has a longer runtime.
The discipline text partly prevents these losses, since it tells the agent that a
long-running job is normal (\ktBothLost{} lost with both parts against
\ktToolLost{} with the library part alone, $p=\ktPToolVsBoth$). Inside
AutoDS-Tools the same four cells lost at most one trial of 24.

\paragraph{Finding 7} Appended to Terminus-2, the file leaves accuracy unchanged
and leaves \ktDiscLost{} of 24 trials without a submission with its
discipline part, \ktToolLost{} of \ktToolTrials{} with its library part and
\ktBothLost{} of 24 with both. For the discipline part, deleting two
paragraphs about training time restores every trial. The effect of an
instruction file depends on the harness.

\subsection{The same file from a working script}
\label{sec:abl-script}

The two AutoDS-Tools columns of Table~\ref{tab:mlab} ablate the whole file
over MLAgentBench, where every task starts from a working script. On
\texttt{clrs}, \texttt{fathomnet} and \texttt{feedback} the cell without the
file scores in every attempt, and the cell with the file submits nothing in
any attempt, every attempt exhausting the agent budget. Only
\texttt{ogbn-arxiv} gains by more than the spread of either cell
($\mlabOgbnOff$ to $\mlabOgbnOn$). On \texttt{amp-parkinsons} the cell
with the file is worse, and the remaining tasks do not change beyond their
spreads. The file replaces a
decision that the supplied script had already made. The two benchmarks
differ in tasks, metrics and budgets as well as in starting point, so we
compare the two outcomes without pooling them.

\paragraph{Finding 8} The file that improves every TabReD task leaves no
submission on \mlabWiped{} of \mlabRepeated{} MLAgentBench tasks and helps
on one. What differs is whether a working default was already in place. The
count is a lower bound on the cost of the file, since the repair loop of
AutoDS-Tools absorbs part of it (Section~\ref{sec:friction}).

\subsection{Container image}
\label{sec:abl-image}

Running Terminus-2 on the enriched image built for AutoDS-Tools changes the
mean normalised score by $\stimageDiff$ (interval $\stimageLo$ to
$\stimageHi$, $p=\stimageP$). Across all 24 trials the agent used none of
the three libraries the image pre-installs and wrote gradient boosting by
hand, as on the stock image.

\paragraph{Finding 9} Pre-installing libraries that no instruction names
changes the mean normalised score by $\stimageDiff$, inside the noise of the harness, and the agent uses none of them.

\subsection{Language model}
\label{sec:abl-language-model}

We replaced the language model under each harness and kept tasks, image
and instructions unchanged. Under AutoDS-Tools the smaller
\texttt{gemma-4-26b-a4b} changes the normalised score by
$\stautodsSmallDiff$ and \texttt{glm-4.7} by $\stautodsBigDiff$ (interval
$\stautodsBigLo$ to $\stautodsBigHi$). Under
Terminus-2 the smaller model gives $\sttermSmallDiff$ and \texttt{glm-4.7}
$\sttermBigDiff$, almost all of it from one task: on
\texttt{sberbank-housing} the stronger model replaced gradient boosting with
a random forest and lost $46\%$ of RMSE. Under Terminus-2 the price of a sweep
of 24 trials varies \axPriceSpread$\times$ across the three language models. Reliability moves in
opposite directions for the two harnesses. Under Terminus-2 trials without a
submission fall from \axTerminusSmallLost{} of 24 to \axTerminusBigLost{} as
the language model grows. Under AutoDS-Tools they are lowest at the model the
system was built with (\axAutoDSRefLost{} of 24) and rise on both sides
(\axAutoDSSmallLost{} of 24 and \axAutoDSBigLost{} of
\axAutoDSBigTrials{}), because a stronger model makes the six agents
exchange more text within the same hour.

\paragraph{Finding 10} Replacing the language model, which changes the API
cost of Terminus-2 \axPriceSpread$\times$, moves accuracy by $\stautodsSmallDiff$ to
$\stautodsBigDiff$ under AutoDS-Tools and, under Terminus-2, by up to
$\sttermBigDiff$ through a single task. It moves reliability in opposite
directions for the two harnesses.
