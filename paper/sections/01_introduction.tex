\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Remaster, 3 October 2026.  One idea per paragraph: context, gap, what we
% do, the three findings, contributions, outline.

Large language models have changed automated machine learning. Systems
built on them plan experiments, write and repair code, read execution logs
and revise their own solutions, which classical AutoML frameworks, searching
a fixed space of pipelines, could not do~\citep{nikitin2022fedot,gijsbers2024amlb}.
Such a system has three parts that can be varied independently. The
\emph{backbone} is the language model. The \emph{harness} is the code
around the model: its tools, control flow, memory and prompts (the 2024--2025
literature says scaffold~\citep{chan2025mlebench,hu2026beyondmodel}). The
\emph{instruction file} is the standing text practitioners add to tell the
agent which libraries to use and how to work, now packaged as an agent
skill~\citep{li2026skillsbench}.

Which of the three parts the accuracy comes from is unmeasured. Papers in
the area introduce one system and report that it improves on a previous one,
with the backbone, the harness, the instructions and the evaluation protocol
all changing at once, so the published ablations
disagree~\citep{toledo2025aira,brilliantov2026harness}. Agentic AutoML
systems are also rarely measured against the baseline a competent
practitioner would reach for, gradient boosting with tuned hyperparameters;
where gradient boosting appears, it is usually at its defaults.

This paper measures the three parts separately. We fix the backbone to one
open-weight 31B model, \texttt{gemma-4-31b-it}, and run three harnesses that
span the design space of agentic AutoML (Figure~\ref{fig:systems}):
\textbf{FEDOT.LLM}, a workflow with four fixed stages in which the model
configures the FEDOT AutoML library; \textbf{AutoDS-Tools}, a system of six
specialised agents that communicate through written reports; and
\textbf{Terminus-2}, the minimal single-agent harness of
Terminal-Bench~\citep{merrill2026terminalbench}, which gives the model a
shell and an execution loop. AutoDS-Tools ships with an
expert instruction file of about 5\,000 characters that names the library to
use and prescribes a training discipline; we switch that file on and off,
split it into its two parts, and hand it to Terminus-2. We also swap the
backbone for a smaller and for a larger model. Every configuration runs in
the Harbor framework~\citep{harbor2026}, which records each attempt, error,
token and second. Accuracy is read on the eight tasks of
TabReD~\citep{rubachev2024tabred}, real-world tabular tasks with temporal
splits, against the eighteen Optuna-tuned reference methods published with
the benchmark, on a scale where 0 is the weakest and 1 the strongest
published method per task. MLAgentBench~\citep{huang2024mlagentbench}, where
every task starts from a working script, and three published scientific
studies give two further starting points.

Three findings organise the paper.
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{The harness alone moves tabular accuracy by about as much
  as the choice of attempt.} With the instruction file removed, the three
  harnesses lie within $\spanArch$ of each other on the normalised scale,
  the range a single harness covers over its own three attempts is
  $\attRangeTerminus$ to $\attRangeFedot$, and no pairwise difference
  between harnesses reaches significance over the eight tasks. The harness
  does determine reliability, time and coverage: AutoDS-Tools spends
  $\costRatioMin\times$ the mean wall-clock of Terminus-2, and FEDOT.LLM
  can attempt tabular tasks only.
\item \textbf{The instruction file is where the accuracy comes from, and it
  is conditional on the harness and on the starting point.} Inside
  AutoDS-Tools it lifts the normalised score from $\nAutoDSOff$ to
  $\nAutoDS$, improving all eight tasks ($p=\stfileBothP$); the part that
  names the library carries the gain and alone reaches $\nGridTool$.
  Appended to Terminus-2 the same file leaves accuracy unchanged and ends
  \ktDiscLost{} of 24 trials without a submission with its discipline part,
  \ktToolLost{} of \ktToolTrials{} with its library part and \ktBothLost{}
  of 24 with both; for the discipline part, two paragraphs about training
  time are the cause. On MLAgentBench, where a working script is supplied,
  the whole file leaves \mlabWiped{} of \mlabRepeated{} tasks without any
  submission.
\item \textbf{No configuration searches over hyperparameters, and that gap
  separates all of them from the strongest published method.} AutoDS-Tools
  as shipped is level with tuned LightGBM ($\nLGBM$), its library-only cell
  level with tuned XGBoost ($\nXGB$), and the strongest published method, an
  MLP-PLR ensemble at $\nBest$, is out of reach of every configuration. The
  traces show one hand-written gradient-boosting configuration per trial and
  no search. FEDOT.LLM, whose backend searches over models within its
  budget, beats every published method on one task and is level with
  Terminus-2 over the eight.
\end{enumerate}

The contributions are: (i) a controlled comparison of three agentic AutoML
harnesses on one open backbone against tuned reference methods, with repeats
and paired statistics (Section~\ref{sec:results}); (ii) an ablation of the
instruction file into its two parts, across two harnesses and two starting
points, which explains why published ablations of instruction files
disagree (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}); (iii) ablations of the container
image and the backbone under the same protocol; (iv) a trace analysis that
locates the mechanism behind each effect (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism});
(v) an external check on three published scientific tasks; and (vi) the
release of all tasks, instruction files, traces and scripts.

The finding bears on how agent leaderboards are read. Terminal-Bench~2.0
introduces Terminus-2 as a neutral scaffold so that a leaderboard over it
compares models~\citep{merrill2026terminalbench}, and recent work on coding
agents reports that elaborate harnesses add little over a minimal one once
the model is strong~\citep{brilliantov2026harness,hu2026beyondmodel}. On
tabular data with a mid-size open model we find the same for the harness and
the opposite for the instruction file: the file moves the result by
$\layerWorthNorm$ on the normalised scale; a backbone swap that changes the
bill \axPriceSpread$\times$ moves it by $\stautodsSmallDiff$ to
$\stautodsBigDiff$ under AutoDS-Tools and, under Terminus-2, by up to
$\sttermBigDiff$ through a single task. A leaderboard over one harness
therefore measures its instructions as much as the model.
