This section reports the three harnesses as they are used: AutoDS-Tools with
its instruction file, FEDOT.LLM with its built-in library and Terminus-2
with the task statement alone. These comparisons change several parts of
the scheme at once, so each subsection states which hypothesis it bears on.
Section~\ref{sec:ablations} then changes one part at a time.

\subsection{TabReD: the harnesses among the tuned methods}
\label{sec:leaderboard}

Figure~\ref{fig:leaderboard} places the three harnesses among
the eighteen published methods, and Table~\ref{tab:leaderboard} in \ref{app:raw} gives the raw
values. AutoDS-Tools with its instruction file reaches a mean normalised
score of $\nAutoDS$, on par with tuned LightGBM and below tuned XGBoost
($\nXGB$) and the strongest published method, an MLP-PLR ensemble
($\nBest$). It is ahead of Terminus-2 ($\nTerminus$) on
\stshipAutoDSvsTerminusWins{} of 8 tasks. On the remaining one,
\texttt{sberbank-housing}, its RMSE lies inside the range of the three
Terminus-2 trials. Section~\ref{sec:ablations} shows how much of this lead
comes from the instruction file.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig_leaderboard.pdf}
\caption{The three harnesses among the eighteen tuned TabReD methods.
(a)~Average rank over the eight tasks with 95\% bootstrap intervals over
tasks. (b)~Each task on the normalised scale, with whiskers spanning the

\label{fig:leaderboard}

FEDOT.LLM ($\nFedot$) is level with Terminus-2 on average and differs from
it on single tasks in both directions. Its results follow the default
pipeline of its backend. For classification FEDOT starts from an ensemble of
CatBoost, XGBoost and LightGBM joined by logistic regression, and for
regression from a random forest. It searches only if fitting that pipeline
leaves enough of its 1\,200~s budget, which occurred on two tasks of eight.
The default ensemble gives the best ROC-AUC of any method on
\texttt{ecom-offers}, and the default random forest ranks 18th to 20th of 21
on the four largest regression tasks. On \texttt{sberbank-housing}, where
the search ran and tuned a LightGBM, FEDOT.LLM ranks third of 21.

This comparison changes the harness, the instruction file and the model
builder together. Its order agrees with \ref{hyp:builder}. AutoDS-Tools,
whose file hands the modelling to a library that searches and tunes, ranks
first among the three. FEDOT.LLM also uses a library and ranks high only
where the library starts from a boosting ensemble or runs its search.
Section~\ref{sec:abl-harness} compares the harnesses without the file
(\ref{hyp:harness}).

\subsection{MLAgentBench: two harnesses from a working script}
\label{sec:mlab}

Table~\ref{tab:mlab} compares Terminus-2 with AutoDS-Tools on the eight
MLAgentBench tasks that repeat on our hardware, with three trials per task
under one job and one verifier. The comparison between harnesses uses the
AutoDS-Tools column without the instruction file, since Terminus-2 carries
none. AutoDS-Tools is more accurate on \headWinsAutoDS{} of the
\headTasks{} tasks and Terminus-2 on \headWinsTerminus{}. On the other two
the systems are level: on \texttt{identify-contrails} both score near zero,
and on \texttt{amp-parkinsons} their means are within 5\%. The median trial
of Terminus-2 takes $\mlabMedTerminus$ minutes against $\mlabMedAutoDS$ for
AutoDS-Tools, and Terminus-2 leaves more trials without a submission,
\headMissTerminus{} of 24 against \headMissAutoDS{}. The spread within a
cell is large on this benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:stats}), so only the win
count and the submission counts are read as results.

Both systems run without the instruction file here, so the comparison bears
on \ref{hyp:harness}. The win count favours AutoDS-Tools within a large
spread. The clear differences are in time and reliability,
which is where \ref{hyp:harness} expects the harness to show.

\subsection{Three scientific case studies}
\label{sec:cases}

Three published studies with an author-reported baseline were turned into
Harbor tasks (Figure~\ref{fig:casedata}). Maize grain yield in the
Genomes-to-Fields trials~\citep{kick2023yield} is predicted for
location-years the model has never seen, with the authors' own split. Exit
state of jobs on the Fugaku supercomputer~\citep{antici2025fdata} is
predicted from what the scheduler knows at submission time. A constant
predictor scores $\caseFdataFloor$. Glass-transition temperature of polymers
in OpenPoly~\citep{wang2025openpoly} is predicted from a PSMILES string.
F-DATA and OpenPoly use random 80/20 splits, so their author figures come
from a different split: close in the case of F-DATA, distant in the case of
OpenPoly, where the authors used their full dataset.

The task text was written for AutoDS-Tools and is the most prescriptive
condition in the paper. It names
LightAutoML~\citep{vakhrushev2021lightautoml} as the required library,
supplies a working snippet, lists the columns that leak the target,
describes the split and states the author baseline as the number to beat.
Every system has three trials per case. AutoDS-Tools ran on that text
with its own instruction file off, since the text already names the library.
Terminus-2 ran on that text and on the same text with the library section
removed. FEDOT.LLM ran on the two tabular cases with a 40-minute backend
budget, its library being built in. Figure~\ref{fig:cases} shows every
trial and Table~\ref{tab:cases} gives every row.
\input{tables/cases}

Given the task text without the library section, Terminus-2 exceeded the
author baseline on maize, the one case with the authors' own split (Pearson
$r$ of $\caseTermPlainMaizeMean$ against $\caseAuthorMaize$). It also
exceeded the author figure on F-DATA ($\caseTermPlainFdataMean$ accuracy
against $\caseAuthorFdata$). A trial took one to five minutes. On OpenPoly it chose RDKit~\citep{rdkit}
fingerprints and descriptors under XGBoost, the featurisation the authors
published.

With the library section in the text, Terminus-2 ended all
\caseTermPrescTotal{} trials without a submission, against
\caseTermPlainLost{} of \caseTermPlainTotal{} without it. The mechanism is
that of Section~\ref{sec:abl-terminus}: the prescribed LightAutoML call takes
$\caseLamaFitFdataMin$ to $\caseLamaFitMaizeMin$ minutes on these tables, and
the agent declared the task complete after $1.5$ to $5.5$ minutes with the
fit still running. AutoDS-Tools completed all nine trials on the same
text, its repair loop absorbing the wait. It is on par with Terminus-2 on
F-DATA and OpenPoly and below it on maize. On maize both agents validated
on random splits of the training rows, which mix location-years, and saw
$R^2$ of $0.65$ to $0.70$. On the test set, whose location-years are absent
from training, the random forest and the gradient-boosting model that
Terminus-2 wrote in its two scored trials reached $0.34$ and $0.41$, and the LightAutoML ensemble of AutoDS-Tools at most $0.07$. FEDOT.LLM is the best system on
F-DATA, with balanced accuracy $\caseFedotPlainFdataBalancedAccuracy$
against $\caseTermPlainFdataBalancedAccuracy$ for Terminus-2 and
$\caseAutoDSFdataBalancedAccuracy$ for AutoDS-Tools.

The case studies change the task text, so they check the hypotheses outside
the benchmarks. The library section that AutoDS-Tools completes leaves every
Terminus-2 trial without a submission, as \ref{hyp:interaction} expects.
FEDOT.LLM is the best of the three on the classification task F-DATA with
its default boosting ensemble, as on TabReD. Maize limits \ref{hyp:builder}.
There the tuned ensemble of AutoDS-Tools transfers worse than the
hand-written models of Terminus-2. Both agents validated on rows that mix
location-years, so the search likely tuned to a validation set unlike the
test set.

\subsection{Cost and wall-clock}
\label{sec:cost}

\input{tables/cost}
A TabReD trial costs under a cent for any harness (Table~\ref{tab:cost}),
so the binding budget is wall-clock. AutoDS-Tools spends
$\costRatioMin\times$ the mean wall-clock of Terminus-2, whose cell ran on
another machine (Section~\ref{sec:design}), and \rerunCut{} of
its 24 trials were cut by the one-hour limit (\rerunLost{} without a
submission). FEDOT.LLM spends $\minFedotTabredMin$ to
$\minFedotTabredMax$ minutes per task, almost all of it in the backend,
with a few thousand model tokens per trial. On
MLAgentBench the mean of Terminus-2 is driven by a few long trials: its
median trial takes $\mlabMedTerminus$ minutes against a mean of $19.1$, and
one trial accounts for $84\%$ of the cost of its cell.

Cost bears on \ref{hyp:harness}. The $\costRatioMin\times$ ratio above is
with the instruction file on. Without the file, on the same image and the
same server, the median TabReD trial of AutoDS-Tools takes $\gridNeitherMin$ minutes against
$\termEnrichedMedMin$ for Terminus-2, about $\costRatioNoFileMed\times$.
