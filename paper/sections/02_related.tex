\paragraph{Agentic AutoML systems} LLM-based systems for machine-learning
tasks range from workflows with fixed stages, where the model fills in
configurations or code templates, to multi-agent systems with planner,
coder and reviewer roles, to single agents in an open execution
loop~\citep{trirat2025automlagent,li2024autokaggle,guo2024dsagent,yang2025rdagent,kulibaba2025kompeteai,fang2025mlzero,lapin2025lightautods}.
They also differ in where knowledge about libraries and methods comes from:
the parametric memory of the backbone, retrieved competition
write-ups~\citep{guo2024dsagent,kulibaba2025kompeteai}, or API-level
knowledge embedded in the decision process~\citep{fang2025mlzero}. Most
papers present one system and report an improvement over a previous one,
with the backbone, the harness and the evaluation protocol all changing
between papers. Their ablations disagree: on MLE-bench the search structure
is the largest contributor in one system and has no measurable effect in
another, and the ranking of memory against search flips between
studies~\citep{toledo2025aira}. Such disagreement is expected when factors
are removed one at a time on different backbones and task sets and the
factors interact. We hold the backbone, the tasks and the environment fixed
and cross the factors.

\paragraph{How much the harness matters} Evidence from coding agents points
in two directions. With strong frontier models, elaborate harnesses add
little: a single session of a minimal coding agent matches four open-source
ML-engineering harnesses under the same backbone and time
budget~\citep{brilliantov2026harness}, and the advantage of complex harnesses
on issue repair shrinks as the model
improves~\citep{hu2026beyondmodel,kapoor2024agents}. With weaker models or
harder tasks, the harness is a large hidden variable: aggregate scores span
$23.8$ points across harnesses evaluated identically~\citep{harnessbench2026},
harness choice induces a 40$\times$ spread in tokens per solved
task~\citep{scaffoldeffect2026}, and with GPT-4o a purpose-built scaffold
earns medals in $8.7\%$ of competitions against $0.8\%$ and $4.4\%$ for two
general-purpose ones~\citep{chan2025mlebench}. GRACE-DS~\citep{tsymbalov2026graceds}, the
closest work in domain, structures the interaction of LLM-driven AutoML
agents on tabular tasks and finds that structure compresses the spread across
eight frontier backbones of 80B to 1.6T parameters. We vary the harness under
one 31B backbone, the complementary design, and read the spread against
tuned reference methods.

\paragraph{Instruction files and skills} Procedural instructions mounted
into a harness are now a distinct object of study. Curated skills lift pass
rates by 16.6 points across 18 model--harness configurations, and the gain
depends on the harness~\citep{li2026skillsbench}; repository-level context
files for coding agents give no general improvement and add 20\% to
inference cost~\citep{gloaguen2026agentsmd}; retrieved external knowledge
lowered the medal rate of one data-science agent on its easiest
tasks~\citep{yang2025rdagent}; expert-provided knowledge raised the solve
rate of science agents by two points~\citep{chen2025scienceagentbench}. In
tabular AutoML, dataset descriptions in the prompt were the first such
intervention~\citep{hollmann2023caafe}. These studies switch the whole file
at once. We split the file AutoDS-Tools ships with into the part that names
the library and the part that prescribes a training discipline, measure each
inside two harnesses and at two starting points, and find that the two parts
act through different mechanisms (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). An automatic
harness-evolution study reports that the system prompt contributes little
compared with tools and memory~\citep{harness2026prompt}; our file is a
prompt-level intervention, and its two parts together gain less than the
sum of their separate gains.

\paragraph{Evaluation against tuned baselines} Agentic AutoML papers that
compare against classical methods almost always compare against defaults;
GRACE-DS normalises against an untuned HistGradientBoosting model. The AutoML
benchmark literature reports average rank and a normalised score against
tuned frameworks, with the Friedman and Wilcoxon tests of
Dem\v{s}ar~\citep{gijsbers2024amlb,demsar2006statistical}. TabReD publishes
eighteen methods, among them XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost, each
Optuna-tuned and averaged over fifteen seeds on temporal
splits~\citep{rubachev2024tabred}; we adopt that table as the reference and
the benchmark's conventions for ranks and normalised scores.
