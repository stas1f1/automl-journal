\subsection{Factors and configurations}

The design tests the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:hyp-scheme}. Each factor
changes one part of the scheme while the others stay fixed. Five factors
are varied:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Harness}: control, execution loop and memory (\ref{hyp:harness}). FEDOT.LLM, AutoDS-Tools, Terminus-2.
    \item \textbf{Instruction file}: knowledge about the procedure, whose library instruction also sets the model builder (\ref{hyp:builder} and~\ref{hyp:interaction}). The library and the discipline instruction of Section~\ref{sec:file}, each on or off, crossed in a full $2\times2$ on AutoDS-Tools and on Terminus-2. For Terminus-2 the byte-identical text is appended to the task statement.
    \item \textbf{Language model} (\ref{hyp:interaction}): gemma-4-31b-it in every configuration. AutoDS-Tools and Terminus-2 are also run on a smaller model of the same family, gemma-4-26b-a4b-it, and on a larger one from another family, glm-4.7, because the Gemma family has nothing larger than 31B. In glm-4.7, family and size change together, so the ablation does not separate them.
    \item \textbf{Starting point}: whether the effect of the file depends on what the agent receives (\ref{hyp:interaction}). From scratch, where the agent receives the task statement and the data (TabReD and the case studies), and from a working script, where it also receives a weak training script to improve (MLAgentBench).
    \item \textbf{Container image}: the environment, varied once to separate its effect from that of the file. The benchmark's stock image or the enriched image built for AutoDS-Tools, which adds the libraries its instruction file names. This factor is varied once, on Terminus-2.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmarks, reference methods and metrics}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

\textbf{TabReD}~\citep{rubachev2024tabred} contributes eight real-world
tabular tasks with temporal train, validation and test splits. Three are
classification tasks scored by ROC-AUC and five are regression tasks scored
by RMSE in raw target units. The benchmark publishes a table of eighteen
methods:
XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost~\citep{chen2016xgboost,ke2017lightgbm,prokhorenkova2018catboost},
random forest, linear models, tabular deep networks, ensembles and
retrieval-augmented models. Each is tuned with
Optuna~\citep{akiba2019optuna} on the validation split and averaged over
fifteen seeds. All agents run on
the official splits through one data adapter and are scored with the
official metric. The agents receive the labelled validation split, which
the published methods used for tuning only. Section~\ref{sec:threats}
discusses how this difference affects the comparison. We read every TabReD
result in two ways, as the AutoML benchmark does~\citep{gijsbers2024amlb}:
as the average rank among the eighteen published methods and the agents,
and as a score scaled between two reference results. Our reference results
are the weakest and the strongest published methods. For task $j$ with raw score $s$ the normalised score
is $n_j(s) = (s - w_j)/(b_j - w_j)$, where $w_j$ and $b_j$ are the weakest and
the strongest published values on that task, so that $0$ is the weakest and
$1$ the strongest published method. A value above $1$ or below $0$ lies
outside the published range. We report the mean of $n_j$ over the eight
tasks.

The three outcomes of Section~\ref{sec:outcomes} are measured per
configuration. Reliability is the share of trials with a submission.
Accuracy on a task is the mean over its trials with a submission, and a
trial without one is counted under reliability only. A paired comparison of
two configurations uses the tasks on which both have a submission. Cost is
the wall-clock time and the price of the tokens of a trial.

\textbf{MLAgentBench}~\citep{huang2024mlagentbench} contributes ten tasks
over tabular, text, vision and graph data. Each supplies a working but weak
training script and asks the agent to improve it. The benchmark publishes no
reference table, so its scores stay in each task's own metric. Every
MLAgentBench comparison in the paper is between two of our own runs under
identical conditions.

\textbf{Three scientific case studies} with author-reported baselines test
the systems outside benchmark conditions (Section~\ref{sec:cases}): maize
yield prediction~\citep{kick2023yield}, supercomputer job failure
prediction~\citep{antici2025fdata} and polymer glass-transition temperature
prediction~\citep{wang2025openpoly}.

\subsection{Execution environment}

All runs execute in the Harbor framework~\citep{harbor2026}, version 0.22.
For every trial, Harbor records the submission, the output of the benchmark's
own scorer, the number of agent steps and errors, wall-clock time, token
counts and cost. Almost every cell ran on one server with 18 CPU cores,
96~GB of memory and no GPU, four trials at a time, or two for FEDOT.LLM and
the AutoDS-Tools case studies. A TabReD container is limited to 8 cores and
16~GB. Two Terminus-2 cells ran on other machines with two trials at a time:
the stock-image cell of the main comparison on a co-author's machine and the
enriched-image cell of Section~\ref{sec:abl-image} on a workstation.
Wall-clock times that involve these two cells come from different hardware.
Language models are called through OpenRouter. No job sets sampling
parameters: Terminus-2 and AutoDS-Tools send none, so the defaults of the
serving provider apply, and FEDOT.LLM uses the settings of its own
configuration. The cost of a trial is the one Harbor records. For
gemma-4-31b-it the list price was \$0.09 per million input and
\$0.34 per million output tokens. Each trial has a one-hour limit on TabReD
(the backend of FEDOT.LLM is given 1\,200~s of it for search) and the
benchmark's own limit on MLAgentBench. Terminus-2 runs on the benchmark's
stock image (Python~3.13), AutoDS-Tools on the enriched image (Python~3.12
with LightAutoML and the other libraries its file names), FEDOT.LLM on an
image with FEDOT (Python~3.10). Data, splits, metrics and language model are
identical across configurations. In the main comparison Terminus-2 receives the task statement alone,
FEDOT.LLM uses its built-in library and AutoDS-Tools runs with its
instruction file.

\subsection{Repeats, noise and statistics}
\label{sec:stats}

Every cell was run three times per task. The relative standard deviation
across trials ranges from $\noiseTerminusMin\%$ to $\noiseTerminusMax\%$
for Terminus-2, is below $\noiseAutoDSMax\%$ for AutoDS-Tools, and is zero
on \allIdentFedot{} of eight tasks for FEDOT.LLM. On several tasks the agent
wrote the same script in two trials and obtained a bit-identical result.
On the normalised scale, over every choice of one trial per task, the mean
score of Terminus-2 ranges over $\attRangeTerminus$, that of FEDOT.LLM over
$\attRangeFedot$ and that of AutoDS-Tools over less than $0.01$. We treat
differences between harnesses of that size as inconclusive. On MLAgentBench
the spread is far larger (three trials of one cell span $\mlabOgbnOffLo$
to $\mlabOgbnOffHi$ accuracy on \texttt{ogbn-arxiv}), so there we rely on
cells in which a configuration produces no submission at all.

Comparisons between two configurations on TabReD are paired by
task~\citep{demsar2006statistical}. Figure~\ref{fig:ablations} shows the mean
difference in normalised score with a 95\% bootstrap interval over the eight
tasks (20\,000 resamples) and the number of tasks won by each side. Where
the text calls a difference significant, it is by the exact two-sided
Wilcoxon signed-rank test at $p<0.05$, without a multiplicity correction.
With eight tasks the smallest attainable value is $p=0.008$, when all eight
differences have the same sign. For the same reason the average rank in the
leaderboard orders the methods without separating them statistically: the
Nemenyi critical difference over \nMethods{} methods and eight tasks is
\nemenyiCD{} rank positions. The statistics of every comparison, including
Fisher's exact test on the numbers of trials without a submission, are in
the repository.

\subsection{Coverage and deviations}
\label{sec:coverage}

All three harnesses attempt all eight TabReD tasks. AutoDS-Tools and
Terminus-2 attempt all ten MLAgentBench tasks. FEDOT.LLM accepts tabular
inputs only. Two MLAgentBench tasks, \texttt{cifar10} and \texttt{imdb},
could not be repeated on our hardware (sixteen of sixteen trials exhausted
the budget on CPU training) and are omitted from every table with three
trials per cell. Earlier AutoDS-Tools runs on TabReD used a different data
preparation. They are excluded from every accuracy table and used only for
the repair-loop counts of Section~\ref{sec:friction}. The FEDOT.LLM row uses
the system's full configuration (best-quality preset, tuning on, 1\,200~s
backend budget) with the column types read from the task schema, which the
two agents read from the task text. Two of its 24 trials, one on
\texttt{delivery-eta} and one on \texttt{sberbank-housing}, reached the
one-hour limit without a submission. Later trials on these two tasks are
not counted and are listed in the repository.
